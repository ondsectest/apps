What was observed

  • A word or snippet deleted in the app disappears from the list, as expected.
  • But the app's own sync response still returns those entries, each with its full text and a "deleted" flag, long after deletion.
  • So the content is still stored on the server. It is only hidden in the interface, not erased.
  • Nothing in the app says whether deleted data is kept for a number of days or forever.
  • Secondary: the Dictionary may show only a few words (even with all filters applied), yet the sync response returns many more, all the deleted ones included. The list and the server disagree.

Why it matters

  • People delete things for a reason: a private name, a referral link, a draft they regret. "Delete" should mean deleted.
  • Keeping deleted content with no stated limit is a data-minimisation and retention problem, not just a UI gap.
  • It can undermine trust, and it sits awkwardly with privacy laws that expect erasure and a clear retention period.

Confirm, count down, and actually erase

Proposed fixDictionary, Snippets

In the app

  • Honest confirmation: deleting says it is removed from all devices now and permanently erased from Wispr's servers within a stated period (for example 30 days).
  • Recently deleted: a list that shows each deleted item with a countdown and an Undo, so people can see what is still held and for how long.
  • A Data & Privacy line that states the retention period for deleted words, snippets and dictation, and links to the privacy policy.

On the server

  • After the grace period, the row and its text are deleted, not just flagged. The sync response stops returning it.
  • During the grace period, the entry is returned only so the owner's own devices can show Recently deleted and Undo; its text is not used for anything else.
  • The retention period is documented, and the same for every platform.

Why it is better

  • Delete means delete, on a clear timetable people can see.
  • It aligns with data-minimisation and the right to erasure.

How data-protection laws view this

ContextGlobal

General context for the report, not legal advice. The point that cuts across all of them is the same: keep only what you need, delete on request, and tell people how long you keep it.

Laws that expect erasure and stated retention

  • EU and UK GDPR: data minimisation, storage limitation, and the right to erasure ("right to be forgotten").
  • India DPDP Act 2023: erase personal data when it is no longer needed or consent is withdrawn.
  • California CCPA and CPRA: the right to delete, and a duty to state retention periods.
  • Brazil LGPD: deletion of personal data after the purpose ends.
  • Canada PIPEDA: retain only as long as needed, then dispose.

Where this finding touches them

  • Deleted content kept with no limit works against storage limitation and erasure.
  • No stated retention period for deleted data is itself a transparency gap.
  • A clear "erased within N days" promise, honoured on the server, answers all of the above.
